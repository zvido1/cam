You are analyzing one passage from a commercial lease.

You will be shown:
  - an opaque identifier for the passage,
  - the passage itself,
  - an identifier for a surrounding block of text from the same document,
  - that surrounding text, supplied only to help you interpret the passage,
  - the name of a parameter family.

Your task is to CLASSIFY the passage along the dimensions defined in the output
schema, and to ground every classification in text you quote verbatim.

You are not being asked whether the passage is correct, adequate, favorable,
risky, or material. You are not being asked to choose between passages. You are
not being asked to reach any particular conclusion. You are describing what this
passage establishes, and what role it plays, on the evidence in front of you.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT TO CLASSIFY
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

For the parameter family named below, classify:

1. Whether this passage is genuinely evidence bearing on that parameter family
   at all (topical relevance — not whether it is sufficient, and not whether it
   matches any particular requirement).

2. Whether the supplied text shows that the contractual mechanism named by that
   parameter family operates in this document.

3. What charge basis, if any, the passage establishes the proportion is taken
   against — where the parameter family is one that has a charge basis. Report
   what the text establishes. If the text does not establish a basis, say so.
   If more than one basis is established, report all of them. Do not infer a
   basis that the quoted text does not state.

4. What scope or denominator the proportion is measured against, where the
   parameter family has one.

5. The drafting role of the passage.

6. Whether the passage itself carries the numeric quantity for that parameter
   family, or directs the reader elsewhere for it, or carries neither.

The permitted values for each dimension, and their definitions, are in the
output schema. Use only those values.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
GROUNDING RULES — these are the point of the exercise
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

* Quote verbatim. Every quote you supply must appear character-for-character in
  the text you were given. Do not paraphrase, normalize whitespace, correct
  apparent typos, or elide with ellipses. A quote that does not match the source
  exactly will be discarded.

* Keep candidate quotes and context quotes separate. Quotes drawn from the
  passage go in candidate_citations. Quotes drawn from the surrounding text go
  in context_citations.

* Ground each field individually. In field_support, list the citation ids that
  support your answer for each field. If you cannot point to a quote that
  supports a field, answer "unclear" for that field rather than asserting it.
  An asserted field with no supporting citation will be discarded, so an
  ungrounded guess costs you the field and gains nothing.

* Answer only from the text supplied. The surrounding text is a bounded excerpt,
  not the whole document.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHEN YOU CANNOT TELL — READ THIS BEFORE ANSWERING
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"insufficient_context" and "unclear" are correct, permitted, fully acceptable
answers. They are not failures and they are not penalized. They are also not
preferred — do not reach for them when the text supports a determinate answer.
Classify what the text establishes; abstain where it does not.

In particular:

* If the language that would establish whether the mechanism operates could lie
  outside the text you were shown, answer "insufficient_context". Do NOT answer
  "does_not_support_mechanism".

* "does_not_support_mechanism" means the supplied text affirmatively shows the
  mechanism does not operate here. It does NOT mean "I did not see it in the
  excerpt I was given."

* You cannot conclude that something is absent from the lease. You are seeing a
  bounded excerpt. Absence from your excerpt is not absence from the document.

Guessing is worse than abstaining. A confident answer you cannot quote is the
one outcome this task is designed to exclude.
