WHAT IS CAM
-----------
CAM stands for Constrained Assertion Method. It is the AI framework underlying this
lease analysis tool. Most AI tools produce a single confident answer even when the
evidence is weak or models disagree — they are architected to always give you something.
CAM is built differently.

CAM's core principle: before asserting a conclusion, the system evaluates whether
that conclusion is epistemically justified. If it isn't, CAM says so — either by
qualifying the finding, flagging it for human review, or withholding it entirely.

CAM's core approach:
1. Multiple independent AI models (Gemini, Claude, GPT, Grok) each evaluate every
   provision separately, without seeing each other's answers.
2. CAM preserves disagreement rather than hiding it. If models split on whether a
   clause is a deviation, that split is surfaced — not collapsed into a majority vote.
3. CAM withholds assertions when confidence is insufficient. If the evidence does not
   support a clear finding, CAM says so rather than guessing.
4. Every finding is backed by a structured reasoning chain — you can see why a
   deviation was flagged, not just that it was flagged.
5. Severity (Critical, High, Medium, Low) is assessed separately from detection — a
   provision can be flagged as a deviation but assessed as low risk in context.

CAM has been validated across scientific fact verification, legal contract entailment,
and commercial lease analysis — the same multi-model disagreement framework runs
underneath all of them. It is patent-pending technology developed by Tzvi Daum.


CAM SCORE FORMULA
-----------------
The CAM score is a 0-100 confidence number computed as:

    CAM = 100 × (A × E × R × F)

A — Agreement
    How much the evaluator models agreed with each other.
    1.00 = unanimous (3-0, all models agree)
    0.75 = majority (2-1, one model dissents)
    0.50 = split (1-1, models evenly divided)

E — Evidence Alignment
    How well each model anchored its reasoning in actual document text.
    The score is a weighted average across all evaluators:
    1.00 = explicit text cited (model quoted or directly referenced specific language)
    0.93 = structural inference (model reasoned from overall structure and context)
    0.90 = absence-based (model identified what is missing rather than what is present)
    0.82 = ambiguous (model was uncertain about what textual basis it was relying on)
    0.78 = unverified citation (model cited a section that could not be verified in the text)

R — Reasoning Completeness
    How fully the pipeline ran for this provision.
    1.00 = all stages completed (extraction, evaluation, challenge, severity)
    Lower values if challenge or severity stages were skipped (e.g. unanimous CONFORMS
    provisions skip challenge — this is by design and expected, not a problem)

F — Fragility Penalty
    How much automated rules discount the finding.
    1.00 = no fragility rules fired
    Lower values mean one or more fragility signals were detected, such as:
    - Exception clauses added (RULE-LS-001)
    - Defined term redefinition with cascade effect (RULE-LS-002)
    - Qualifier shift (e.g. "shall" → "may") (RULE-LS-003)
    - Quantitative deviation (amounts, percentages, time periods) (RULE-LS-004)
    - Negation pattern added or removed (RULE-LS-005)
    - Cross-reference dependency (RULE-LS-006)
    - Omission (provision missing from tenant lease) (RULE-LS-007)
    - Obligation direction swap (landlord/tenant responsibility reversed) (RULE-LS-008)


CAM SCORE VARIANTS
------------------
CAM (permissive): Score computed under standard evaluation rules — the default view.
CAM (strict): Score computed under stricter evidentiary thresholds. Models must cite
    more explicit textual support; structural inference and absence-based reasoning
    carry less weight.

A high permissive score with a low strict score means the finding is real but
somewhat dependent on interpretation — the models agree something changed, but
they're relying partly on inference rather than a clean textual anchor.


ASG — ASSERTION SENSITIVITY GAP
--------------------------------
ASG = CAM (permissive) minus CAM (strict).

A large ASG (e.g. 40+) means the finding is highly sensitive to interpretation —
the confidence gap between a lenient and strict reading is wide. This is the most
important signal for deciding whether a lawyer needs to personally review a provision.

    ASG < 15:  Low sensitivity. Finding holds under both interpretations. High confidence.
    ASG 15-35: Moderate sensitivity. Worth a second look but likely solid.
    ASG 35-55: High sensitivity. Human review recommended before acting.
    ASG 55+:   Very high sensitivity. The finding may be correct but the evidence
               base is weak. Do not rely on this finding without independent review.


GOVERNANCE SIGNALS
------------------
The governance signal is CAM's final assertion decision for a provision.

ASSERT_SIGNAL
    High confidence. The finding is solid enough to act on. All evaluators agreed,
    evidence is well-grounded, fragility is low, and both permissive and strict
    scores are strong.

REVIEW_SIGNAL
    Moderate confidence. A human should verify before acting. Models agreed but
    evidence grounding was imperfect, or fragility signals were detected, or the
    ASG is large. The finding is probably real but warrants attorney review.

WITHHOLD_SIGNAL
    Low confidence. CAM declines to assert this finding. Either models significantly
    disagreed, evidence was too weak, or fragility signals made the conclusion
    unreliable. This provision may still warrant review, but CAM cannot responsibly
    assert a deviation.

Note: WITHHOLD does not mean "no deviation." It means "CAM cannot confidently assert
a deviation." A withheld provision should be reviewed by a lawyer if it is material.


EVIDENCE BASIS LABELS (shown in the Audit Trail)
-------------------------------------------------
These labels appear next to each evaluator's verdict in Stage 3 of the Audit Trail.
They show how each model justified its conclusion.

Explicit text
    The model cited a specific clause, section number, or quoted language directly
    from the document. Highest epistemic quality — the reasoning is traceable to
    an exact location in the text. E multiplier = 1.00.

Structural inference
    The model reasoned from the overall structure and context of the provision rather
    than quoting a specific phrase. Valid reasoning but slightly less verifiable.
    E multiplier = 0.93.

Absence
    The model identified that something required by the template is missing from the
    tenant lease. Reasoning from absence is legitimate but inherently harder to pin
    to a specific line. E multiplier = 0.90.

Ambiguous
    The model was uncertain about what textual basis it was relying on, or gave
    mixed signals about its grounding. E multiplier = 0.82.

Unverified citation
    The model cited a section reference that could not be confirmed to exist in
    the extracted text. May indicate extraction gaps or hallucination.
    E multiplier = 0.78.


PIPELINE STAGES (shown in the Audit Trail)
------------------------------------------
Each provision goes through up to 7 stages. The Audit Trail shows exactly what
happened at each stage.

Stage 1 — Extraction (Gemini)
    One Gemini API call extracts and aligns the relevant clause text from both
    the template and tenant lease. This is the raw material for all subsequent
    stages. If extraction is imperfect (truncated, paraphrased, or missing sections),
    integrity flags are shown.

Stage 2 — Rules Check (automated, 0 API calls)
    Eight Python rules scan the extracted text for structural risk patterns:
    exception language, defined term overrides, qualifier shifts, quantitative
    deviations, negation patterns, cross-reference dependencies, omissions, and
    obligation direction swaps. Provisions that trigger no rules and have clean
    text pass triage and skip the expensive later stages.

Stage 3 — Independent Evaluation (3 models in parallel)
    Three AI models (Claude, GPT, Grok) each evaluate the provision independently
    without seeing each other's answers. Each produces a verdict (DEVIATES /
    CONFORMS / UNCLEAR), confidence score, reasoning, key differences, and
    evidence basis label. This is the core of the CAM multi-model architecture.

Stage 4 — Targeted Challenge (GPT, reasoning_effort=high)
    For flagged provisions only. GPT challenges the evaluators' findings, looking
    for hidden dependencies, cascade effects through defined terms, and whether
    the deviation is substantive or merely cosmetic. Returns SUBSTANTIVE_DEVIATION,
    COSMETIC_ONLY, or NEEDS_EXPERT.

Stage 5 — Severity Assessment (GPT)
    For confirmed deviations only. Rates the deviation as CRITICAL, HIGH, MEDIUM,
    or LOW based on legal risk, financial exposure, and practical impact. Includes
    financial impact estimate and recommended action.

Stage 6 — Fragility Scoring (computed, 0 API calls)
    Applies the A × E × R × F formula to produce the CAM score. Computes both
    permissive and strict variants and the ASG. Assigns the governance signal.

Stage 7 — Report Generation
    Produces the annotated DOCX (callout blocks) or PDF (highlights + sticky notes),
    the findings dashboard, and the full audit trail.


PROVISION REFERENCE — THE 18 STANDARD PROVISIONS
-------------------------------------------------
LP-01  Rent & Escalation
       Base rent amount, escalation schedule, CPI adjustments, percentage rent.
       Key risk: changes to escalation caps or base amount.

LP-02  Security Deposit
       Amount, form (cash/LC), conditions for draw, return timeline.
       Key risk: reduced deposit, easier draw conditions, non-standard return terms.

LP-03  Lease Term & Options
       Commencement, expiration, renewal options, extension rights.
       Key risk: shortened notice windows, changed option exercise conditions.

LP-04  Permitted Use
       What the tenant is allowed to use the space for.
       Key risk: broadened use clause, exclusivity conflicts with other tenants.

LP-05  Assignment & Subletting
       Whether tenant can assign the lease or sublet space, under what conditions.
       Key risk: removed landlord consent requirements, changed profit-sharing terms.

LP-06  Maintenance & Repairs
       Who is responsible for what maintenance and repairs.
       Key risk: shifted obligations onto landlord, reduced tenant responsibilities.

LP-07  Alterations
       Tenant's right to make alterations, approval process, restoration obligations.
       Key risk: expanded alteration rights without consent, waived restoration.

LP-08  Insurance
       Required coverage types and amounts for both parties.
       Key risk: reduced coverage requirements, changed additional insured terms.

LP-09  Indemnification
       Who indemnifies whom for what losses and liabilities.
       Key risk: narrowed indemnification scope, added carve-outs, mutual vs. one-way.

LP-10  Default & Remedies
       What constitutes default, cure periods, landlord remedies.
       Key risk: extended cure periods, restricted remedies, added notice requirements.

LP-11  Damage & Destruction
       What happens if the premises are damaged or destroyed.
       Key risk: expanded tenant termination rights, changed rebuild obligations.

LP-12  Condemnation
       What happens if the premises are taken by eminent domain.
       Key risk: changed award-sharing, expanded tenant termination rights.

LP-13  CAM Charges (Common Area Maintenance)
       Calculation of operating expenses passed through to tenant, caps, exclusions.
       Key risk: expanded inclusions, removed caps, changed audit rights.

LP-14  Subordination & Attornment
       Tenant's obligations regarding superior mortgages and new landlords.
       Key risk: weakened non-disturbance protections.

LP-15  Estoppel Certificates
       Tenant's obligation to provide estoppel certificates on request.
       Key risk: shortened response windows, expanded scope of certifications.

LP-16  Holdover
       What happens if tenant stays beyond lease expiration.
       Key risk: reduced holdover rent penalty, converted to month-to-month.

LP-17  Notices
       How official notices must be delivered between parties.
       Key risk: changed addresses, removed certified mail requirements.

LP-18  Governing Law & Dispute Resolution
       Which state's law governs, arbitration vs. litigation, venue.
       Key risk: changed jurisdiction, added mandatory arbitration, fee-shifting changes.


SEVERITY LEVELS
---------------
CRITICAL
    The deviation fundamentally alters the lease economics, transfers major risk to
    the landlord, or could result in significant financial exposure. Requires
    immediate attorney review and likely renegotiation before signing.

HIGH
    The deviation meaningfully changes rights or obligations in a way that could
    have material legal or financial consequences. Should be reviewed and addressed
    before signing.

MEDIUM
    The deviation changes the lease terms in a notable way but the immediate risk
    is limited. Worth discussing with an attorney; may be acceptable depending on
    context and negotiating position.

LOW
    The deviation is minor or cosmetic. The legal and financial impact is minimal.
    Note it for the record but likely acceptable without further negotiation.


WHAT CAM CANNOT DO
------------------
- CAM is not a licensed attorney and does not provide legal advice.
- CAM does not know the negotiating history, business context, or strategic
  priorities behind any specific lease. A deviation flagged as HIGH risk may be
  acceptable given specific circumstances only a lawyer can evaluate.
- CAM cannot guarantee it found every deviation. Provisions that were not selected,
  or that fall outside the standard taxonomy, may contain issues CAM did not review.
- CAM's findings are inputs to attorney review, not substitutes for it. The purpose
  of CAM is to surface what matters and explain the confidence level — the final
  judgment belongs to the lawyer.
- CAM does not access the internet, case law databases, or real-time legal information.
  Its analysis is based solely on comparing the uploaded documents against each other.
