## YOUR TASK

### Phase 1: Build the best case for ALL THREE verdicts.

For each of SUPPORT, CONTRADICT, and NOT_ENOUGH_INFO, construct the strongest possible argument using ONLY what the abstract explicitly states. Cite specific sentence numbers. Rate the strength of each case (strong / moderate / weak / no_case).

You MUST steelman every option. Even if all evaluators agreed on one verdict, build genuine cases for the other two.

### Phase 2: Attempt to eliminate each verdict.

For each verdict's best case, look for fatal flaws:

- **scope_gap**: The claim asserts something general but the evidence is specific (e.g., claim says "often" but abstract tests one case), or vice versa.
- **precision_mismatch**: The claim uses a specific number, threshold, or quantity that doesn't match what the evidence actually states.
- **insufficient_evidence**: The case requires inference beyond what the abstract explicitly says.
- **logical_gap**: The reasoning requires an unstated assumption to connect the evidence to the verdict.
- **direct_contradiction**: The abstract explicitly states the opposite of what this verdict's case claims.

A verdict is KILLED only if its case has a fatal, unrecoverable flaw. Weakness alone is NOT enough — there must be a specific, articulable logical failure.

### Phase 3: Determine survivors.

Which verdicts survived elimination? Recommend the strongest survivor.

## CRITICAL RULES

1. You are NOT a fourth evaluator. You are a stress tester. Build and break cases.
2. Build genuinely strong cases — do NOT strawman any verdict.
3. Kill shots must be specific and falsifiable, not vague concerns.
4. "The evidence doesn't explicitly use this exact word" is NOT sufficient for a kill shot unless the terminology gap is material.
5. NOT_ENOUGH_INFO is hard to kill. Only kill NEI if the abstract unambiguously and completely addresses the exact claim with no scope or precision gaps.
6. Be precise about sentence numbers. Cite which sentences support or undermine each case.
