CRITICAL CONSTRAINT: You do NOT evaluate whether the hypothesis is entailed, contradicted, or not mentioned. You evaluate whether the evaluation PROCESS was structurally sound. A "proceed" recommendation does not mean the verdict is correct. An "escalate" recommendation does not mean the verdict is wrong.

HYPOTHESIS:
{hypothesis_text}

CONTRACT TEXT (with span markers):
{formatted_contract}

EVALUATOR OUTPUTS:
{evaluator_outputs}

CHALLENGE RESULTS:
{challenge_summary}

YOUR TASK:

1. STRUCTURAL VALIDITY: Is the overall reasoning process sound?
   - Did evaluators cite specific contract spans (not just "the contract says...")?
   - Is the reasoning internally consistent? (No "ENTAILMENT because it's not mentioned")
   - Does the evidence challenge identify gaps? If so, does the evaluator's reasoning survive?
   Valid values: "valid", "concerns", "invalid"

2. CONSTRAINT COMPLIANCE: Did evaluators follow their instructions?
   - Did they cite specific spans?
   - Did they check for exception clauses?
   - Did they trace definitions of key terms?
   Answer: true (all complied) or false (violations found)

3. GROUNDING QUALITY: Are evaluators reasoning from contract text or from general legal knowledge?
   - "strong": All evaluators ground reasoning in specific cited spans
   - "adequate": Most reasoning is grounded, minor gaps
   - "weak": Significant reasoning without span support
   - "ungrounded": Evaluators reason from assumptions, not contract text

4. CONSISTENCY ISSUES: Note any logical contradictions found in evaluator reasoning.
   Examples: verdict says ENTAILMENT but reasoning describes absence of relevant clauses;
   evaluator cites an exception clause but doesn't account for it in the verdict;
   evaluator's confidence is "high" but reasoning contains hedging language.

5. CHALLENGE SURVIVAL: Does the reasoning survive the evidence challenges?
   - Did the challenger identify missing spans, negation gaps, or definition gaps?
   - If yes, do the evaluators' verdicts still hold despite those gaps?
   Provide a brief assessment.

6. SPAN OVERLAP ASSESSMENT: Do evaluators who agree on verdict cite similar evidence?
   - "high": Agreeing evaluators cite largely the same spans
   - "partial": Some overlap but also distinct spans cited
   - "none": Agreeing evaluators cite completely different spans (fragile agreement signal)

7. RECOMMENDATION:
   - "proceed": Reasoning process is structurally sound. Move to fragility detection.
   - "flag": Concerns detected but not fatal. Fragility detection should weigh these.
   - "escalate": Critical structural problems. Verdict reliability is in question.

CONSTRAINTS:
- Do NOT render your own verdict on the hypothesis.
- Focus on process quality, not outcome quality.
- Be specific: cite evaluator labels (A, B, C) and span numbers.
