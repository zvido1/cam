### Phase 1: Build the best case for ALL THREE verdicts.

For each of ENTAILMENT, CONTRADICTION, and NOT_MENTIONED, construct the strongest possible argument using ONLY what the contract text explicitly states. Cite specific span numbers. Rate the strength of each case (strong / moderate / weak / no_case).

You MUST steelman every option. Even if all evaluators agreed on one verdict, build genuine cases for the other two.

Legal-specific considerations:
- For ENTAILMENT: Identify spans that explicitly establish the obligation/permission described in the hypothesis. Check that exception clauses don't undermine the entailment.
- For CONTRADICTION: Identify spans that explicitly negate or prohibit what the hypothesis asserts. A mere absence is NOT contradiction.
- For NOT_MENTIONED: Identify whether the contract is genuinely silent on the topic, or whether the hypothesis falls in a gap between clauses.

### Phase 2: Attempt to eliminate each verdict.

For each verdict's best case, determine if there is a FATAL flaw. A fatal flaw must fall into one of exactly four categories:

- **direct_textual_contradiction**: A specific span in the contract explicitly states the opposite of what this verdict claims. Not a technicality — an unambiguous, affirmative statement that negates the verdict's core claim.
- **definitional_exclusion**: A contractually defined term explicitly excludes the scenario described in the hypothesis, and the verdict's case depends on that term covering it.
- **logical_impossibility**: The verdict is internally self-contradictory given the clause structure — e.g., the same span cannot simultaneously support ENTAILMENT and CONTRADICTION.
- **complete_scope_absence**: For ENTAILMENT or CONTRADICTION verdicts only — the contract contains absolutely no language that addresses or could reasonably encompass the hypothesis topic. Not "the contract doesn't use these exact words" but "the contract is genuinely silent on this entire subject."

Any objection that does NOT fall into one of these four categories is a CONCERN, not a kill. Record concerns in strongest_objection but set survives=true and critical_weakness=null.

QUOTE REQUIREMENT FOR KILLS:
For any verdict you mark as killed (survives=false), you MUST include in the critical_weakness field:
- The EXACT verbatim text from the contract that creates the fatal flaw (in quotation marks)
- The span number where this text appears
- A clear explanation of why this text is fatal, not just a concern

Example of a valid kill: "Span 14 states 'The Receiving Party shall not reproduce, copy, or duplicate any Confidential Information' — this directly contradicts the ENTAILMENT case because it explicitly prohibits copying."

Example of an INVALID kill (would be overridden): "Span 14 discusses restrictions on Confidential Information which could be interpreted as limiting copying" — this is vague and does not quote specific fatal language.

If you cannot produce an exact quote that creates a fatal flaw, the verdict SURVIVES. Set survives=true and record your concern in strongest_objection.

CRITICAL CALIBRATION RULES:
- A verdict may be imprecise without being wrong. Contracts routinely use general language that covers specific scenarios without naming them explicitly.
- The absence of a specific term does NOT constitute a kill if general language reasonably encompasses the hypothesis.
- Exception clauses (notwithstanding, provided however, subject to) are concerns unless they DIRECTLY and UNAMBIGUOUSLY negate the verdict's entire basis.
- Inference from general to specific is normal legal reading, not a fatal flaw.
- If you cannot identify a specific span number that creates the fatal flaw, it is not a kill.

### Phase 3: Determine survivors and recommend.

Which verdicts survived elimination? Recommend the strongest survivor. If multiple survive, state your confidence in the recommended verdict.

IMPORTANT: If all 3 verdicts survive, that is a genuine finding -- it signals real ambiguity in the contract language.
